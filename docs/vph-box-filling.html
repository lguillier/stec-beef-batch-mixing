STEC in minced beef · contamination of raw material units

From 60 carcasses to one VPH box

A 1,000 kg VPH box combines meat contributions from 60 cattle. Only a few carcasses may contribute contamination, producing substantial differences in bacterial loads between boxes.

    Boxes compared

      Each new box uses 60 new animal identifiers; one animal's pieces never go to two boxes here, because the R model does not track that allocation.

      Technical details: what the R model computes
      1. Reference piece count. Piece masses are drawn from a triangular distribution (minimum 50 g, mode 500 g, maximum 1,000 g; mean ≈ 517 g) until their cumulative mass reaches 1,000 kg (rNotrm). The number of pieces drawn, Notrm, is the reference count. It is drawn once per simulation run: here Notrm = {NOTRM}.
      2. Pieces per contaminated carcass. For each contaminated carcass, the number of pieces is Poisson with mean Notrm / 60 = {LAMBDA} (about 32 pieces, about 16.7 kg per animal).
      3. Piece masses. Each piece mass is drawn again from the same triangular distribution.
      4. Surface. Each piece gets a surface-to-mass ratio uniform between 0.1 and 0.5 cm²/g; its surface area is mass × ratio.
      5. Expected organisms per piece = carcass load allocated to beef for mincing × piece surface area / 32,000 cm² (the total carcass surface). Pieces from one carcass share its load but differ in mass and surface.
      6. Box total. The expected organisms of all simulated pieces of the box are summed. These simulated contributions are not adjusted to sum to exactly 1,000 kg; the box mass stays 1,000 kg by definition, and meat from uncontaminated carcasses is never generated.

      The R script keeps expected (decimal) numbers. The integer "observed count" shown here is one Poisson draw per piece, added only to show the difference between an expected number and a count you could observe; it is not part of the R output.

      The 3.9% faecal shedding prevalence used upstream is not the carcass contamination prevalence. In the R model the number of contaminated carcasses per box is a binomial draw whose probability comes from the hide-transfer and evisceration equations; the two contaminated carcasses shown here are a demonstration setting.

      In the physical view, uncontaminated animals also drop Poisson(Notrm/60) pieces so the box looks full. That part is schematic: the R model does not create those pieces, and the drawn total is close to, but not exactly, 1,000 kg.

      This is a simplified visualization of the R model. It does not track the allocation of other meat from the same animal to additional boxes.
